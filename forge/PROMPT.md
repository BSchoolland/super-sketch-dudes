You're implementing a character that a player drew into this game. The drawing is at {{DRAWING}}; look at it. {{NOTES}} If the player asked for something interesting do your best to follow it within reason; the rest of these instructions are for the common case:

Start by sending their image to `npx tsx forge/tools/sheet.ts {{DRAWING}} {{WORK}}/cells`, which draws the animation and outputs the cells to that folder (run it in the background, it takes about 40 seconds). Take a look at `forge/exemplar/sword-guy.fighter.js` for how characters work and why they are built that way, in parallel while the animation runs (its cells are in `forge/exemplar/sword-guy/`); `forge/exemplar/fire-wizard.fighter.js` is a second one, a caster. Once it's done, take a look at the images and design the character based on this. Only make down-special a counter if that really fits the character or the player asked for one; otherwise give it something more interesting. Projectiles and strikes can carry a look (colours, textures, or a crop of one of the cells spun as a sprite); see `looks` in the exemplar. Write it as `{{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js`. Balance test briefly using `npx tsx forge/tools/check.ts {{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js {{WORK}}/cells`, look at every move as a player would see it with `npx tsx forge/tools/preview.ts {{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js {{WORK}}/cells` (contact sheets in `{{WORK}}/preview/`; read them and fix anything that looks wrong), and when you're happy with it use `npx tsx forge/tools/deploy.ts {{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js {{WORK}}/cells --name "…" --tagline "…"` to push the character to the live game. Only that fighter file and the cells folder can be uploaded: anything else you change in this checkout stays here.
