You're implementing a character that a player drew into this game. The drawing is at {{DRAWING}}; look at it. {{NOTES}} If the player asked for something interesting do your best to follow it within reason; the rest of these instructions are for the common case:

Start by sending their image to `npx tsx forge/tools/sheet.ts {{DRAWING}} {{WORK}}/cells`, which draws the animation and outputs the cells to that folder (run it in the background, it takes about 40 seconds). Take a look at `forge/exemplar/sword-guy.fighter.js` for how characters work and why they are built that way, in parallel while the animation runs (its cells are in `forge/exemplar/sword-guy/`); `forge/exemplar/fire-wizard.fighter.js` is a second one, a caster. Once it's done, look at every cell next to the drawing before you design anything. The image model gets characters wrong in a few ways: it misreads a part (a head-and-shoulders drawing's shoulders turned into legs), adds something the drawing doesn't have, draws one cell that doesn't look like the character, or forces a pose that doesn't fit it (a paper bag throwing a punch). Anything it added that the moderation rules in `forge/moderate.ts` would block (nudity or sexualised anatomy, gore, a rude gesture or shape) must not ship, not even in a cell no move uses, since every cell is uploaded. Fix a bad cell one of three ways: drop it with `npx tsx forge/tools/drop-cell.ts {{WORK}}/cells <bad cell> <good cell>`, which makes it a copy of the good one (then don't build moves around it); redraw the sheet by running sheet.ts again with `--note "…"` telling the image model what to do differently; or, if the image model can't get this character right, use `--from-drawing` to make all nine cells the drawing itself and let the pose tracks do the moving. Look at the cells again after any redraw. Say in your final message which cells you dropped or redrew and why. Then design the character based on the cells. Only make down-special a counter if that really fits the character or the player asked for one; otherwise give it something more interesting. Projectiles and strikes can carry a look (colours, textures, or a crop of one of the cells spun as a sprite); see `looks` in the exemplar. Write it as `{{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js`. Balance test briefly using `npx tsx forge/tools/check.ts {{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js {{WORK}}/cells`, look at every move as a player would see it with `npx tsx forge/tools/preview.ts {{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js {{WORK}}/cells` (contact sheets in `{{WORK}}/preview/`; read them and fix anything that looks wrong), and when you're happy with it use `npx tsx forge/tools/deploy.ts {{WORK}}/{{ID}}.fighter.js {{WORK}}/cells --name "…" --tagline "…"` to push the character to the live game. Only that fighter file and the cells folder can be uploaded: anything else you change in this checkout stays here.
